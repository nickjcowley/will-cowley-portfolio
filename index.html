Metal-cutting CNC mill

2026 to 2027, in progress

My first CNC router could cut wood well but flexed too much for metal. So I designed a second machine from scratch in Fusion 360, aimed at stiffness: a fixed steel bridge with a moving table, profile linear rails and ballscrews.

I chose welded steel box section, left over from building work, for the frame. The columns will be filled with a sand and oil mix to damp vibration. I considered a cast-concrete frame and epoxy granite, but rejected them as impractical and expensive when the steel is already rigid.

The bench and frame are welded, the columns and bridge are primed, and the rails and ballscrews are being fitted. The electronics come next, and I aim to finish the machine during my gap year.

Drives
Closed-loop NEMA 34 steppers; SFU2005 ballscrews on X and Y, SFU1605 on Z
Spindle
2.2 kW, air-cooled
Tools
Fusion 360, plasma cutter, MIG and TIG welding, lathe, mill, taps and dies

Man-overboard recovery system

A-level Design Technology project, 2025 to 2026, grade A*

Most yacht safety kit only works if the person in the water can help themselves. My client, an experienced skipper with a 31 ft yacht in Cardiff Bay, wanted a way to recover someone who is unconscious or exhausted. I designed, made and sea-tested a two-part system.

Read the full project (PDF, 42 pages)

A hook on a pole threads a rope through the casualty's lifejacket loop, so they are secured without having to grab anything. Then a weighted mesh sling is lowered under them and the main halyard and winch roll them up the side of the boat.

I iterated the threading mechanism through three laser-cut prototypes and used Fusion 360 FEA to check the 3 mm aluminium. A prototype later broke exactly where the simulation predicted. I made two versions of the hook: one I sand-cast myself from a 3D-printed pattern, and one CNC-machined with advice from Whitehouse Machine Tools on design for manufacture.

To test it, I jumped into Cardiff Bay in an auto-inflating lifejacket and played an unresponsive casualty. Setup took 2 minutes 30 seconds and I was lifted the 1.2 m to deck level. The system is now kept aboard the yacht.

On the water: 2 min 30 s setup, 1.2 m lift, up to 70 kg
Processes

Model rocket, RGS Worcester rocket club

Team project, 2025: UK Rocketry Challenge (UKROC), sponsored by Airbus

Our school team designed and built a rocket for the UKROC regional competition, where the rocket has to carry an egg and bring it back undamaged. I designed and 3D printed the nose cone and helped run the launches.

The rocket combined 3D-printed parts with laser-cut plywood rings holding the motor tubes. A flight computer deployed the parachute and logged height, speed and time to apogee.

On our first test flight the rocket veered sideways. The team enlarged the fins to make it more stable, and our competition launch was a success.

First CNC router

2024 to 2025, funded by my Arkwright Scholarship

A V1 Engineering LowRider 2, built from an online design. I assembled the mechanics, wired the motors, set up Fusion 360 CAM and a G-code sender, and used it to make engraved signs and wooden wallets.

It cut wood well, but metal showed its limits: backlash in the belts, printed parts that flexed, and a heavy router overloading the Z axis. Adhesive workholding also failed on metal. Each of these shaped a decision on my second machine.
